1 · The structure

Six parts, about 200 words

This is the layout of the class model (Max Mustermann) and the shopping centre report. Use exactly these five headings: they are safe, the teacher expects them, and you can’t copy the task with them.

Date: 13 November 2026

Introduction

The aim of this report is to present the results of a survey on …, to analyse why … and to make recommendations on how the school / company could …. The report is based on a survey conducted among 1,100 young people aged 14–21 / a chart published by ….

Results

According to the survey, the biggest item is the most popular option, chosen by just under a third of respondents (31%). This is followed by item 2 (x%) and item 3 (x%). A significant number of respondents …, while around one in ten …. In contrast, only a small minority … (x%).

Analysis

Several factors may explain these results. One possible reason for this is that …. Another important factor is …. This suggests that ….

Recommendations

In order to improve the situation / attract more …, it would be advisable to …, as reason. Furthermore, the school / company should consider …-ing. Another possible measure would be to ….

Conclusion

In conclusion, main finding. However, with main recommendation, the school / company could result.

3 · Your organic food report (8/11)

Every mistake, and the rule that stops it happening again

Your teacher wrote “Overall, well-structured!”, “Good use of linking words!” and “Overall, good use of language!”. The structure is fine. These are the points that cost marks.

Teacher1 · Introduction: analysis and recommendations missing

“Analysis & Recommendations missing”

YouThe aim of this report is to summarize data on organic food consumption. The report is based on a pie chart from the UK Food Agency. BetterThe aim of this report is to present data on organic food consumption by age, to analyse why young people buy comparatively little organic food and to make recommendations on how to attract younger customers. It is based on a pie chart published by the UK Food Agency.

Rule: the first sentence names all three jobs: present + analyse + recommend. Then the source.

Teacher2 · Prompt lifting

“Prompt lifting! Don’t use the wording from the instructions!”

YouEating Habits of People my AgeBetterAnalysis

Rule: never copy a chunk of 3+ words from the task. Use the five standard headings. When you mention a task point in a sentence, say it in your own words: people your age → young consumers / teenagers / 14- to 18-year-olds; attract young customers → appeal to a younger audience.

Teacher3 · Personal pronouns

“Try to avoid personal pronouns!” (circled: I)

YouIn order to attract more young customers, I would recommend offering student discounts. BetterIn order to attract more young customers, it would be advisable to offer student discounts. or… the company should consider offering student discounts.

Rule: no I, me, my, we, our, you anywhere in the text (only after “From:”). Use it would be advisable / beneficial to, the company should consider + -ing, or the passive Discounts should be offered.

Teacher4 · Results: more linking words

“More linking words could be included at this stage.” (circled: and)

YouIn contrast, people under 29 account for only 15% of consumption and the youngest group (9–18) consumes just 5%. BetterIn contrast, consumption drops sharply among younger people: those under 29 account for only 15%, while the youngest group (9–18) consumes just 5%.

Rule: no two numbers joined only with and. Use followed by · while · whereas · in contrast · by comparison · similarly · on the other hand. Aim for one linking word in every Results sentence.

4 · Phrase bank (your Task 9 sheet)

The phrases, sorted by section

★ = learn these first. One or two per section is enough, and use each phrase only once.

Introduction

  • The aim of this report is to present … , to analyse … and to make recommendations on …
  • The report is based on a survey conducted among …
  • The purpose of this report is to … · This report examines …
  • This report presents the results of …
  • This report was written in order to …
  • The results of the survey are presented below.

Referring to data

  • According to the survey, …
  • The results show that … · The data indicates that …
  • The findings suggest that … · The statistics reveal that …
  • Based on the information collected, …
  • The chart / graph / table illustrates that …

Describing statistics

  • The most popular option is … , followed by …
  • A significant number of respondents … · only a small minority …
  • A majority of / A large proportion of students …
  • Just under a third … · Just over half … · Around a quarter … · About one in ten …
  • In contrast, … · By comparison, … · Similarly, … · On the other hand, …

Analysis: explaining reasons

  • Several factors may explain these results.
  • One possible reason for this is that …
  • This may be due to … · This could be because …
  • Another important factor is … · This indicates that …
  • This suggests that … · It is likely that …

Recommendations

  • In order to improve the situation, …
  • It would be advisable to … · It would be beneficial to …
  • One possible solution would be to …
  • The school / organisation should consider + -ing …
  • Perhaps it would be useful to …
  • Another possible measure would be to …
  • On the sheet but avoid: I would (strongly) recommend … (personal pronoun!)

Linking & conclusion

  • Adding: Furthermore, · In addition, · Moreover,
  • Contrast: However, · Nevertheless, · while · whereas
  • Result: As a result, · Therefore, · For this reason,
  • Conclusion: In conclusion, · To sum up, · Overall, · In summary,
  • Based on the results, … · It can therefore be concluded that … · The report suggests that …

Reporting what people said (Task 6)

Use a reporting verb: said, stated, reported, indicated, claimed. After a past verb, shift the tense back:

  • 29% said, “I want to be an athlete.” → 29% stated that they wanted to become professional athletes.
  • 15% said, “I would like to work with animals.” → 15% reported that they would like to work with animals.
  • Present tense also works, with no backshift: The survey shows that most teenagers want … Pick one style and keep it.
5 · Numbers into words

Never write a number on its own

Write the phrase and put the exact figure in brackets: just under a third of respondents (31%). Don’t start a sentence with a number: the sample report’s “70 percent stated…” is better as “Around 70% of respondents stated…”.

6 · If the chart shows change over time

Trend language (your sheet)

Only needed for line or bar charts with years. Two patterns cover everything: verb + adverb (rose sharply) and adjective + noun (a sharp rise).

Example sentences to copy the pattern from

  • The number of young visitors rose sharply from 400 to 900 between 2020 and 2024.
  • There was a slight decrease in sales in 2023, after which they levelled off.
  • Screen time reached a peak of 6 hours a day in 2021 and has declined moderately since then.
  • Gun sales increased by 15% (= the size of the change) to 2 million (= the end point).

8 · Model report

The shopping centre report: what to copy, what to polish

Copy this from the printed sample

  • Header with To / From / Subject / Date.
  • The five headings in the right order.
  • Intro gives the survey size and age group (1,100 people aged 14–21).
  • Results go from the biggest (96%) to the smallest (60%).
  • Analysis gives reasons; Recommendations give concrete ideas (healthier food, cafés, a cinema).
  • Linking words: Moreover, Furthermore, However, while, Even though, In conclusion.

Polish small things in the sample

  • “Closely followed by shopping for brands which was selected by 85 percent.” has no verb of its own. Write: This is closely followed by shopping for brands (85%).
  • “70 percent stated…” at the start of a sentence → Around 70% of respondents …
  • “the findings on a survey” → the findings of a survey; “ways how to attract” → ways to attract.
  • The intro names results + reasons + suggestions. Good, that is the model for your intro.

Here is the same task written with everything from this page. About 220 words without the header. Yellow = phrases from your sheet.

To: The Centre Manager
From: Leon
Date: 13 November 2026
Subject: Making the shopping centre more appealing to young people

Introduction

The aim of this report is to present the results of a survey on why young people visit the shopping centre, to analyse them and to make recommendations on how to increase the number of young visitors. The report is based on a survey conducted among 1,100 people aged 14 to 21.

Results

According to the survey, meeting friends is by far the most common reason (96%). This is closely followed by shopping for brands (85%). A large majority also come to eat out (70%), while around two thirds use the free internet (66%). Free entertainment is the least popular reason (60%).

Analysis

These figures suggest that the centre is mainly a social meeting point rather than just a place to shop. One possible reason for this is that teenagers have few other places to meet at little cost. It is also likely that the affordable fast-food restaurants attract young visitors.

Recommendations

In order to appeal to a younger audience, it would be advisable to create more comfortable seating areas. Furthermore, the centre should consider adding healthier food options and trendy cafés. Another possible measure would be to organise regular events such as concerts.

Conclusion

In conclusion, young people visit the centre mainly to socialise. More meeting spaces and a wider range of food and entertainment could therefore attract many new visitors.

9 · In the test

How to spend the time

0–3 minRead the task twice. Underline: who it is for (→ To:), the source and the number of people, and every bullet point (each one needs its own section).
3–8 minPlan on the side: the 3 biggest + 1 smallest figure with their phrases · 2 reasons · 2–3 recommendations. Turn task words into your own words now.
8–35 minWrite with the template. Header → Intro (aim + analyse + recommend + source) → Results → Analysis → Recommendations → Conclusion.

What three jobs must the first sentence of the Introduction name?

Present the results · analyse them · make recommendations. Then the source.

What are the five headings, in order?

Introduction · Results · Analysis · Recommendations · Conclusion

Rewrite: “I would recommend offering student discounts.”

It would be advisable to offer student discounts. / The company should consider offering student discounts.

What is “prompt lifting”, and how do you avoid it?

Copying wording from the task. Use the standard headings and paraphrase the task points: “people your age” → “young consumers / teenagers”.

31%, 52%, 66%, 10%, 3% in words?

just under a third · just over half · around two thirds · one in ten · a small minority

Join these with a linking word: “Older people buy 30%. Teenagers buy 5%.”

Older people account for 30%, while / whereas teenagers buy just 5%. (or: In contrast, teenagers …)

Two phrases to start the Analysis?

Several factors may explain these results. · One possible reason for this is that …

“The number rose a lot.” Make it formal (verb + adverb, then adjective + noun).

The number rose sharply / considerably. · There was a sharp / considerable rise in the number.

“29% said, ‘I want to be a doctor.’” Report it.

29% stated that they wanted to become doctors.

that

fewer or less? “___ students cycle than walk.” · “Young people spend ___ money.”

What goes in the Conclusion?

Main finding + main recommendation in 1–2 sentences. Nothing new. Start with “In conclusion,” / “To sum up,” / “Overall,”.
